## 1. What to Expect From the Process

- **Recruiter/screening call** — English communication, career walkthrough, motivation for freelance/Toptal work.
- **Skills review / online assessment** — timed, scenario-based questions covering product sense, prioritization, metrics, and business analysis (not pure trivia — expect "what would you do" scenarios).
- **Live interviews with senior PMs or Product Directors** — product sense, user story mapping, metrics analysis, sometimes a whiteboard-style product design question.
- **Test project / task-based round** — a realistic mini-assignment (e.g., define a roadmap, write a PRD, analyze a metric drop) done semi-independently and reviewed.
- **Culture fit interview** — values, remote-work readiness, collaboration style.

**General tips:**
- Think out loud — the *process* is being evaluated, not just the final answer.
- Always clarify the problem/goal/constraints before diving in.
- Structure every answer (framework → analysis → recommendation → trade-offs).
- Keep answers time-boxed; don't over-explain simple points.

## 2. Product Sense / "Design a Product" Questions

**Prompt pattern:** *"Design an ATM for kids." / "Improve product X for user segment Y." / "How would you build [product] for [market]?"*

### CIRCLES Method (Lewis C. Lin)
1. **C**omprehend the situation — restate the problem, ask clarifying questions (who, what, constraints, goals, existing solutions).
2. **I**dentify the customer — pick a specific, realistic user segment (don't try to serve everyone).
3. **R**eport the customer's needs — what job are they trying to get done?
4. **C**ut, through prioritization — list several needs, choose the most impactful one to solve.
5. **L**ist solutions — brainstorm 3–5 ideas, briefly.
6. **E**valuate trade-offs — pros/cons, cost vs. impact, feasibility.
7. **S**ummarize recommendation — pick one, state why, note next steps/metrics to validate.

### Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)
Frame the user's need as: *"When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."* Keeps you focused on the underlying job, not just feature requests.

### Persona quick-build (when none is given)
Name, age range, context of use, tech comfort, primary goal, biggest frustration. Keep it to 2–3 sentences — don't over-invest in fiction.

## 3. Metrics & Analytics Questions

**Prompt pattern:** *"Pick an app and describe its core metrics." / "DAU dropped 20% last week — what do you do?"*

### Metric selection frameworks
- **AARRR (Pirate Metrics):** Acquisition → Activation → Retention → Referral → Revenue.
- **HEART (Google):** Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task success — good for UX-heavy products.
- **North Star Metric:** the single metric that best captures the core value delivered (e.g., Airbnb = nights booked, Slack = messages sent between teams).

### Diagnosing a metric drop/spike — structured approach
1. **Clarify** the metric definition and time window (is it real or a measurement/logging bug?).
2. **Segment**: by platform, geography, new vs. returning users, cohort, channel.
3. **Internal factors**: recent releases, bugs, pricing changes, outages, seasonality.
4. **External factors**: competitor moves, market/regulatory shifts, holidays.
5. **Funnel breakdown**: which specific step in the funnel changed?
6. **Hypothesize → prioritize → validate** with data before recommending action.

### Common metric types to know cold
- **Leading vs. lagging indicators**
- **Activation rate, retention curves (D1/D7/D30), churn, LTV, CAC, LTV:CAC ratio**
- **Conversion rate, funnel drop-off**
- **NPS vs. CSAT vs. HEART**

## 4. Prioritization Frameworks

| Framework | Best for | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **RICE** | Comparing many feature candidates | Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort |
| **ICE** | Quick, lightweight scoring | Impact × Confidence × Ease |
| **MoSCoW** | Scoping a release | Must have / Should have / Could have / Won't have |
| **Kano Model** | Understanding feature-satisfaction relationship | Basic (must-be) / Performance / Delighter (excitement) attributes |
| **Value vs. Effort matrix** | Quick visual triage | Plot ideas on a 2x2, prioritize high-value/low-effort first |
| **Weighted scoring** | Multiple stakeholder criteria | Score each option against weighted criteria (strategic fit, revenue, risk, etc.) |

**When asked "how would you prioritize your roadmap?"** — name a framework, but always tie it back to: business goals → user impact → engineering cost/risk → dependencies.

## 5. Strategy & Business Analysis

- **SWOT** — Strengths, Weaknesses (internal); Opportunities, Threats (external).
- **Porter's Five Forces** — competitive rivalry, supplier power, buyer power, threat of substitutes, threat of new entrants. Useful for market-entry or positioning questions.
- **OKRs** — Objective (qualitative, inspiring) + 2–4 Key Results (quantitative, measurable). Distinguish from KPIs (ongoing health metrics vs. OKRs' time-boxed targets).
- **Business model basics** — revenue model, cost structure, unit economics (CAC, LTV, margin), TAM/SAM/SOM sizing.
- **Go-to-market** — positioning, target segment, channel strategy, pricing model.

### Market sizing (Fermi estimation) — quick structure
1. Define the question precisely (geography, timeframe, unit).
2. Pick a top-down or bottom-up approach and state which.
3. Break into estimable components (population → % relevant → frequency → price).
4. Make explicit, reasonable assumptions out loud.
5. Do the math, sanity-check the final number against a known reference point.

## 6. Execution, Agile & Working With Engineering

- **Agile/Scrum basics**: sprints, backlog grooming, story points, velocity, standups, sprint review/retro.
- **User story format**: *"As a [user], I want [goal], so that [benefit]."* Include acceptance criteria.
- **PRD components**: problem statement, goals/non-goals, user stories, success metrics, scope (in/out), risks, timeline/dependencies.
- **Trade-off communication with engineering**: always frame scope/time/quality trade-offs explicitly rather than just pushing deadlines.
- **Technical fluency** (Toptal often has a technical-leaning PM track): be ready to discuss APIs, databases at a conceptual level, how a web request flows client → server → DB, and basic system design vocabulary (latency, scalability, caching) — you don't need to write code, but you should be able to have an informed conversation with engineers.

## 7. Behavioral / Culture-Fit Questions

**Prompt pattern:** *"Tell me about a time you disagreed with an engineer." / "How do you handle conflicting stakeholder priorities?"*

### STAR Method
- **S**ituation — brief context.
- **T**ask — your specific responsibility.
- **A**ction — what *you* did (not the team) and why.
- **R**esult — quantified outcome + what you learned.

**Common themes to prepare 1–2 stories for:**
- Prioritizing under conflicting stakeholder demands
- Handling a failed launch or missed metric
- Influencing without authority (getting eng/design buy-in)
- Making a decision with incomplete data
- Managing scope creep or a tight deadline
- Working async/remote across time zones (relevant to Toptal's model specifically)
